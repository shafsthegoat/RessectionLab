## 1. The outcome I want

I want to build something a neurosurgeon would find useful for inspecting and rehearsing difficult decisions, and something Medivis engineers would respect as technically substantial. Optimize toward preserving neurological function and vascular supply while achieving appropriate tumor removal. Operationalize that aspiration through inspectable, evidence-supported endpoints; do not claim patient-outcome improvement from a simulated score.

Replace the idea of a surgical path as a line through a tumor with a **stateful surgical strategy**: patient positioning and exposure; scalp preparation and incision; bone opening with actual cutting trajectories and angles; dural/cortical access; changing bimanual instruments; tissue manipulation and removal; information gathering; hemostasis; responses to complications; and a justified decision to continue, pause, change approach, leave residual, or stop. ### Non-negotiable data policy — real observations only

**Never use synthetic data.** This applies to training, pretraining/fine-tuning for this project, validation, performance benchmarks, acceptance demonstrations and clinical-looking example cases. Exclude generated patient images or anatomy, procedural patients, virtual lesions, cross-person anatomical composites, fabricated functional responses or outcomes, simulated operative trajectories, model-generated transitions, and synthetic augmentation such as invented deformations, noise corruption, mixup or generative modality completion. Do not use synthetic examples to fill a rare complication, missing subgroup or failed data-access requirement.

Eligible evidence includes actual patient acquisitions and recorded procedures, source-linked human annotations, actual human-specimen measurements with appropriate rights, and genuine device metrology/calibration records. Nonhuman evidence can inform a mechanism's literature rationale but cannot become human patient data. Physical phantom experiments cited in the literature are engineering precedents, not substitutes for the real biological evidence required here. Provenance-preserving preprocessing—coordinate conversion, registration, resampling, cropping, intensity normalization and feature extraction from acquired records—is allowed when documented, with originals retained; it must not invent another patient, acquisition, event or outcome. Model-estimated segmentations, tracts and other derived quantities remain estimates, never measured ground truth or fabricated training labels. Human-reviewed, source-linked annotations must record their actual review status.

**RL means learning from recorded real experience under this rule.** A scan combined with simulator-generated actions, transitions or rewards does not become a real recorded trajectory. Use offline RL only when actual actions, time-ordered observations, next observations, censoring and defensible observed endpoint/reward support exist. Do not generate simulator rollouts for RL, use search-generated “expert” demonstrations, invent missing intermediate states, or train on counterfactual outcomes. No online exploration on patients or clinical hardware is authorized. Real scans without action histories remain valuable for anatomy, representation learning and component evaluation, but must not be counted as direct RL experience. Insufficient eligible trajectories is a data-acquisition problem, not permission to relax this rule.

The platform may still compute geometric plans, numerical mechanics and explicitly labeled model predictions on real case inputs for inspection and rehearsal. These are **model outputs**, not additional observations, training experience, observed alternative outcomes, or validation ground truth. Keep them out of all training and RL replay buffers and empirical evidence totals. Store reproducible rehearsal logs separately with explicit prediction provenance. Pure algebra, coordinate identities and conservation checks verify software; they do not supply synthetic patient datasets or satisfy real-case acceptance gates. A hypothesis from a simulator remains a hypothesis until checked against suitable real evidence.

Audit the pretrained perception/policy/component weights used by the experimental system and their training-data lineage as well as raw files. Do not introduce weights trained on synthetic data into the compliant learning/evaluation pipeline. If lineage is unknown, mark eligibility unresolved and use a verified alternative or train on eligible real records; do not silently certify unknown weights. Preserve historical experiments and source citations, including studies using simulation, as historical evidence only. Quarantine incompatible artifacts from new training and benchmark inputs. No agent may rename generated data “augmentation,” “calibration,” “counterfactual,” “digital twin” or “real-anatomy RL” to bypass the restriction. Changing this rule requires an explicit instruction from me.

**This iteration must expand actual data use.** Use as many eligible, nonduplicate, task-relevant real datasets as can be acquired and processed within available rights and resources. Maximize unique people, sites, acquisition protocols, clinically relevant conditions and recorded decisions, not download count or repeated versions of the same patients. Use each dataset for the task its observations support. Small subsets are ingestion checks, not the final training ambition; move from verified pilots to the full eligible TRAIN portions and document every exclusion or capacity limit. More datasets are not automatically better: measure the effect of adding each source and retain honest negative results.

Feedback must name observable behavior—tool separation, travel, delayed support-tool positioning, clearance or activation history—and expose its evidence. [Giglio et al.][R05] supports investigating AI-informed human coaching; [Fazlollahi et al.][R06] motivates monitoring unintended changes in other skills. Neither establishes this rewind-and-retry design.

**First demonstration and acceptance:** reproduce one error, inspect the explanation, retry an alternative, and assess a different scenario with feedback disabled. Separate geometric explanation correctness from educational benefit. A subsequent authorized human study should assess retention and transfer using blinded review and multiple outcomes independent of the coaching score. Use a source-linked real recorded error for the observed replay, and label the retry as a model proposal; do not call an authored trace an expert benchmark or infer safe tissue force from an uncalibrated proxy. Human study participation and review remain real external gates; do not fabricate them or contact participants without authorization.

### Objective 6B — Preserve mechanical and thermal history across actions

For retraction, compare continuous holding, release/repositioning and distributed contact while matching the achieved working corridor. Retain contact footprint, orientation, pressure distribution and time history. [Roca et al.][R20] motivates separating total force from edge pressure; [Hongo et al.][R21] motivates time-dependent response. These findings do not supply a universal safe pressure or a patient injury threshold.

For bipolar interaction, compare activation sequences with the same total activation time but different spacing, dwell and cooling. Retain local temperature after deactivation, repeated heating and irrigation effects; sealing, vessel patency and thermal exposure are separate outputs. [Sakatani et al.][R22] provides an experimental lead for parameter dependence, not clinical device settings.

**First demonstration and acceptance:** use appropriately sourced real human-specimen or patient measurements, held-out actual force-relaxation or temperature traces, and independently checked time/mesh convergence. Device calibration records may support instrument parameters but cannot establish biological response. For thermal models, account for energy and cooling. Reopen relevant mechanics gates before claiming calibrated response. Report measured and predicted loading/temperature histories separately; an injury model needs its own evidence. Integrate accepted models into the common action clock. If measurements are missing, pursue eligible data and retain an unresolved calibration gate instead of a phantom or generated dataset fallback.

## 7. Vessel injury, bleeding, perfusion and field visibility

Go beyond a collision penalty. Use interaction geometry, vessel class, contact/activation history, and declared tissue mechanics to produce intact, damaged, leaking, compressed, occluded, or transected states. Contact need not cause rupture; occlusion can cause harm without visible bleeding. Keep vessel identity and cumulative injury state across steps and branches.

Build a tractable pressure/resistance or other justified reduced circulation model for supported vessel graphs. Account for arterial versus venous behavior, upstream supply, drainage, downstream perfusion and uncertain collateral routes. Do not infer flow parameters, wall strength, collateral sufficiency, or tissue tolerance solely from a segmentation radius. Use measured parameter ranges and disclose any remaining model assumption. Missing collateral or boundary-condition evidence cannot be filled with invented vessels, patient measurements or training examples. Include both major-vessel injury and small critical-branch injury, with different downstream consequences.

Couple bleeding to operative field fluid, visibility, interventions and systemic loss. Maintain explicit mass/volume accounting for circulating blood, escaped blood, pooled field blood, suctioned blood, irrigation and CSF. Suctioning previously escaped blood must not subtract it from the circulation twice. Coagulation/compression/other supported hemostatic actions can change bleeding and patency separately. Clearing the view does not automatically restore circulation or repair injury.

Return separate endpoints for blood loss/rate, vessel injury/patency, regional perfusion deficit/exposure duration, field visibility, non-target tissue effects and task progress. Stable systemic BP does not rule out local ischemia. Cessation of bleeding does not certify vessel preservation. A large tumor-removal reward must never compensate away a declared catastrophic vascular violation.

### Objective 7A — Explain why similar vessel interactions can have different downstream effects

Compare genuinely acquired vessel networks or real timepoints with documented differences in collateral connectivity and sufficiently comparable local geometry. Evaluate a proposed compression/occlusion as a labeled model prediction and show redistribution and dependence on measured or unresolved boundary conditions. Seek a recorded occlusion without visible bleeding where available for empirical comparison; do not invent a paired vascular network or claim observational differences identify a causal treatment effect. Use the experimentally constrained [Bollu circuit model][R07] as a reduced-model precedent; its rat imaging and measured-flow constraints do not justify predicting human perfusion from segmentation radius alone.

**First demonstration and acceptance:** use real segmented vascular graphs with documented topology/coverage and actual flow or pressure measurements where available. Independently check conservation, disconnected components and pressure/flow consistency; algebraic circuit identities are software checks, not synthetic case benchmarks. Compare predictions against withheld real measurements and assess sensitivity over empirically supported parameter ranges without generating training records. Do not fabricate a collateral loop or terminal branch to fill the suite. Persistent vessel identity/topology must survive replay. Flow changes, perfusion exposure and tissue infarction remain distinct endpoints; unresolved real flow data keep the corresponding validation gate open.

### Objective 7B — Find and control a bleeding source while preserving a usable view

Start from an actual recorded operative cavity/bleeding episode with lawful access and enough spatial, temporal and intervention evidence for the intended claim. Reconstruct supported observations without inventing hidden leak locations or unrecorded actions. In rehearsal, examine whether one instrument can maintain source access while the other performs a supported intervention, explicitly labeling all unobserved alternatives as predictions. Separate source-identification time, visible working time, evacuation, continued leakage, source control, vessel flow and tissue contact. Clearing a pool while losing the source is a visibility failure. [Huang et al.][R08] supplies a simulation/physical-cavity engineering precedent, not an eligible patient dataset or evidence of hemostasis.

**First demonstration and acceptance:** acquire at least two eligible real recorded cases with differing cavity/source configurations and sufficient measurements. Compare supported controller proposals on their acquired geometry and evaluate observed sequences against actual video, instrument and fluid records. Report which alternatives have no observed outcome. Check fluid accounting, source-detection delay, continued visibility and encounters only where independent measurements support them. Do not manufacture leak locations, fluid traces or tissue labels. Unavailable paired interventions prevent a causal controller-performance claim. Integrate with Objective 7A as model rehearsal, keeping predicted perfusion distinct from recorded physiology and infarction.

## 8. Causal vitals, compounds and the anesthesia teammate

Own each state in one module and establish units, shared simulation time, update order, event causality and observation delays. The coupling chain must be inspectable:

`tool interaction → local tissue/vessel state → bleeding/patency/field fluid → systemic loss counted once → cardiovascular/respiratory and drug effects → regional cerebral state → delayed/noisy monitors → decisions`.

Support evolving HR, BP/MAP, circulating volume, oxygenation/ventilation, hemoglobin/oxygen delivery, temperature and anesthetic state at an explicitly stated fidelity. Include relevant CO2 relationships and distinguish measured monitors from latent physiological quantities. Blood loss does not imply an instantaneous proportional hemoglobin fall. Open skull/dura and brain relaxation cannot be treated as an unchanged closed-box ICP problem; avoid a single universal CPP/ICP rule or safety threshold.

Represent compounds/interventions through administration records, route, units, bolus/infusion timing, distribution/clearance, effect-site delays, interactions and observations where supported. Research hypnotics, opioids, inhaled agents, neuromuscular blockade, vasoactive agents, fluids/blood products, osmotic agents, steroids, antiseizure drugs and local anesthetics as relevant. Their timescales and endpoints differ. Paralysis is not anesthesia, and a list of named drugs beside animated vitals is not pharmacology.

Assess Pulse, BioGears and narrower mechanistic models against the exact intended scenarios. Pin versions and inspect actual implementation as well as methodology docs. Earlier reviews found scope limitations around interactions, extreme dosing, concentration behavior, and internal/brain hemorrhage; verify those version-specific concerns before using an engine. Do not assume general physiology validation establishes neurosurgical patient prediction.

Initially implement an explicit scripted/protocol-constrained anesthesia teammate with actions, latency and feedback, while the surgery agent can pause/request assessment. Any learned anesthesia control is a separate bounded research task with independent validation, not an unrestricted dosing action added to the surgical policy. Include uncertainty, delayed recovery, physiological reserve, ventilation/airway problems and scenarios where a reassuring monitor misses a local problem. Use actual recorded administration histories and sourced mechanistic parameters for calibration/evaluation; keep proposed interventions as labeled model outputs, never generated RL experience or unsourced clinical prescriptions.

## 9. Planning, RL and risk profiles

Define a versioned dynamic episode alongside the preserved geometric benchmark. Use hierarchical, duration-aware actions: exposure/strategy choice → tool pair and local objective → fine motion/activation → observation/reassessment. The action vocabulary must include information acquisition, clearing a field, tool exchange, pause, escalation, stopping with residual, and recovery maneuvers. Make options interruptible with persistent state.

The environment should be partially observable. Policies receive available images, visible anatomy, tracking/monitoring observations and history, with their delays and uncertainty. Hidden vessels, exact latent perfusion, future images, and simulator truth must not leak through observations, action masks, rewards used as inputs, preprocessing, or proposal generation. Oracle policies are permitted only as clearly labeled bounds.

Optimize a vector of clinically motivated, model-supported endpoints with explicit constraints or lexicographic priorities. Report residual tumor by compartment, healthy-tissue effects, functional evidence/exposure, vascular state, blood loss, physiological burden, visibility, uncertainty, time and instrument changes. Include risk distributions/tail behavior and Pareto alternatives under declared assumptions. Model-predicted event frequency is not a measured clinical risk probability. Cosmetic preferences and speed cannot trade away hard constraints.

Compare scripted expert-inspired rules, geometric/observation-aware search or receding-horizon planning, imitation where demonstrations exist, and a justified RL approach. Match information, supported actions, eligible real-case sampling and execution-time opportunities; report training cost separately from online cost. Consider learned proposal ranking or next-observation selection when they address measured bottlenecks. Do not force RL to win or hide stronger classical baselines.

Actually run a reproducible **offline RL** experiment when the section 1 eligibility gate is met. Preserve the actual recorded transition manifests, observation/action/reward definitions, configurations, seeds, learning curves, parameter updates and checkpoints; evaluate independently on eligible withheld real records. A frozen imitation checkpoint, dataset catalog, real scan with simulated actions, or component pretraining run is not an RL training result. If the recorded data cannot support a valid RL problem, leave RL completion explicitly blocked and advance acquisition, annotation and supported component learning. If RL underperforms, diagnose and report it while keeping the best supported planner available.

Define success, adverse events and censoring independently of training reward. Include immediate-stop and reckless-removal diagnostics; ensure “always stop” cannot masquerade as a useful completed resection, while a justified stop can be preferable to proceeding. Separate injury-prevention and complication-rescue benchmarks. Ending an episode must not erase accumulated injury or pending delayed consequences; follow them through a declared observation horizon.

Hold out patients, relevant operators/trajectories, sites, acquisition periods and source families with real observed variation. Audit dataset/checkpoint overlap before split construction, including derived releases and pretrained weights. New random seeds are not new anatomy or clinical validation. Lock evaluation configurations before tuning; retain excluded, unsupported, timeout and failed cases in the denominator. Assess observed segmentation/registration errors, missing-vessel coverage, measured tissue variability and recorded tracking failures; do not fabricate perturbation datasets. Report uncertainty and negative results.

Each recommended strategy must export: input/evidence identity; supported exposure and cut geometry; ordered instrument actions with poses, duration and activation; required observations and preconditions; possible interruptions/recovery; anatomy and physiological state over time; uncertainty assumptions; endpoint vector; rejected alternatives; and stopping rationale. A path is “recommended” only within its declared model and evidence—not a claim of globally optimal surgery.

### Required training protocol — broad real data with a valid decision problem

Use [Levine et al.'s offline-RL review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01643) as a methodological starting point for learning from previously collected experience and the challenges of limited data coverage; it supplies no neurosurgical dataset or clinical validation. Select an algorithm only after inspecting the actual available action/observation structure. Do not assume that an image before surgery plus an image after surgery reveals the intervening action sequence.

1. **Establish eligibility before optimization.** Each recorded transition needs source/subject/episode, actual action and timestamp/duration, then-available observations, subsequent observations, recorded endpoint support, missingness and censoring. Unknown actions stay unknown. Align modalities on real clocks and record synchronization uncertainty. Define reward transparently from observed quantities; a computed scalar from actual recorded measurements may be a declared surrogate, but a simulated deficit, invented intermediate reward or alternative outcome is ineligible. Terminal observed outcomes must not be copied backward as newly observed step outcomes.
2. **Use all compatible qualified sources.** Pool real records only where the action semantics, timing, observations and target task are compatible. Use separate task heads or separate experiments for incompatible decisions; document why a source cannot supply surgical RL. Prevent a large source from silently dominating, track patient/source-balanced sampling, and report per-source examples and gradient contributions. Add newly qualified cohorts through versioned mixtures; preserve every earlier checkpoint and data manifest.
3. **Build baselines and learn incrementally.** Compare recorded clinician behavior, behavior cloning where appropriate, supported rules/search, and justified conservative offline RL under the same information/action scope. Train shared perception components on the widest eligible real datasets without holdout leakage, then attribute their contribution separately from policy learning. Generated expert labels, model-based data expansion, synthetic augmentation and simulated exploration remain excluded. Baseline comparisons without recorded alternative outcomes must be labeled model comparisons or descriptive analyses, not observed superiority.
4. **Measure what added data changes.** Record loss/return proxies, actual optimizer changes, task outcomes where observed, action support, calibration, abstention and source-specific errors. Run a prespecified small-to-large patient/cohort learning curve and leave-source-out development analyses. Compare single-source and pooled training at a controlled budget where feasible, then report the best supported larger-data run with its added cost. Use the final untouched evaluation only after selection is fixed.
5. **Bound retrospective claims.** Inspect behavior-policy coverage, missing confounders, censoring and reward sensitivity. Report effective sample/support diagnostics and uncertainty for any off-policy estimate; decline it when assumptions or action overlap fail. Learned actions outside supported data must trigger abstention or separately declared planning assumptions. Neither a high offline value estimate nor successful model rehearsal demonstrates patient benefit, general safety or the outcome of an unobserved alternative operation.

**Deliverable:** an actual training run from each admitted compatible real trajectory source, a pooled run when multiple sources qualify, an auditable contribution table and independent evaluation. The source count is an observed result, not a number to satisfy with relabeled imaging cohorts. A dataset that harms performance still belongs in the documented comparison; exclusion from the final model needs a recorded, development-only reason. Lack of accessible real trajectories must remain visible in the problems register and completion status.

### Objective 9A — Acquire information when it can change a decision

Represent measurement availability, duration, cost, noise, failure and observation likelihood explicitly. Maintain a belief over the supported unknowns; an observation updates that belief rather than exposing simulator truth. Select a landmark, mapping task, viewpoint or available image update according to expected decision benefit. Include a highly uncertain region whose measurement would not change the action and a less uncertain boundary that could. Use [POMCP][R16] as one candidate planning method, with simple observation-aware search as a required comparator; its non-surgical evaluations do not validate surgical information selection.

**First demonstration and acceptance:** use actual paired observations with a measured displacement, two proposed removal choices, STOP and the genuinely recorded measurement locations/timepoints. Compare fixed-schedule, uncertainty-based, decision-value and no-update planning only where the record supplies comparable information. Release each observation at its real availability time; do not generate noisy measurements, repeated scans or alternative mapping responses. Measure decision changes, model-predicted encounters, observed correspondence error and actual observation cost separately. Include genuinely uninformative or failed observations where recorded. Without comparable acquisition/action support, present a descriptive decision-replay study and leave superiority of adaptive acquisition unproved.

Extend this narrowly to awake mapping using actual recorded task trials: task identity, baseline performance, stimulation/recording modality, location, repetitions, observed errors, timing and cooperation/signal quality. Keep stimulation findings, ECoG activity and task performance distinct. [Sanai et al.][R13] and [Erez et al.][R14] motivate patient/task-specific evidence; they do not establish a response generator or public access to participant-level trials. Use genuine recorded disagreement or unreliable responses where available. Compare fixed/adaptive proposals only to the extent actual observations permit, without inventing responses to unperformed tasks. Negative findings, poor cooperation and ECoG silence cannot establish safety. Missing task-level records block response-model fitting and adaptive-policy validation.

### Objective 9B — Learn where to spend expensive simulation calls

Use a learner to allocate candidate previews, search depth or scenario evaluations according to expected decision improvement per computation. Build on the current action ordering and budget accounting instead of discarding the stronger search baseline. The preserved four-case comparison motivates this quality/cost question but does not prove a general speed advantage; its timings and representations have known confounds. [Hay et al.][R18] provides the metareasoning framework. Existing DAgger, action ordering and Pareto/CVaR support are baselines, not the proposed new result.

**First demonstration and acceptance:** compare fixed-order search, existing actor ordering and adaptive allocation with identical observations, candidate availability, preparation and budget rules. Use bounded candidate sets on real acquired cases for exhaustive geometric model comparisons, without treating model-best actions as training labels or clinical ground truth; use withheld real cases for transfer. Count allocator inference, state copying, previews, verification and failures in online cost, and report training cost separately. Require either better supported decision quality at matched cost or equivalent quality with fewer computations, with repeated balanced timing where a speed claim is made. Every selected action must retain full geometric/constraint checks; learned pruning is not a safety certificate. Train the allocator only from eligible real recorded supervision and measured execution-cost logs; generated search rewards or pseudoexpert trajectories are not an allowed shortcut. This bounded ML experiment complements the recorded-experience RL requirement.

### Objective 9C — Make branch comparisons expose their causal assumptions

Branch before a decision into continue, measure, change approach and stop. Identify the earliest event responsible for each endpoint difference, then examine one named assumption such as registration bias, execution error or observation noise over its empirically supported range. Separate recorded patient attributes and disturbances from hypothesized action-dependent effects; freeze and disclose the modeled coupling between branches. [Oberst and Sontag][R17] shows why the causal mechanism matters: matching transition distributions or reusing a seed alone does not identify meaningful counterfactual outcomes.

**First demonstration and acceptance:** reproduce the identical observed prefix of a real case, then compare proposed branches under named, empirically grounded model assumptions. Use actual measured error/timecourse alternatives where available rather than authored patient scenarios or resampled disturbance datasets. Reproduce snapshots, identify assumption-dependent ranking reversals and retain predicted pending effects after STOP. Export model versions and clearly distinguish recorded facts from every branch prediction. No alternative branch outcome enters training or counts as an observed patient outcome. A retrospective trajectory cannot validate an unobserved alternative policy.

## Appendix D. Focused objective sources and five-subagent review provenance

This register accompanies the October 6, 2026 synthesis. Preserve these literature attributions, but apply the later real-data-only rule in section 1: simulation, synthetic or phantom experiments cited here are methodological background, not authorized datasets or eligible training trajectories. Sources already present in Appendices A/C are retained here when the focused objectives rely on them; they are not counted as newly discovered literature. Access descriptions state what was available during the review, not a promise that every full text will remain accessible. Retrieve and check the relevant methods, figures, parameter definitions and licensing before implementation. An abstract is a research lead, not sufficient parameter provenance.

The five workstreams were **planning/learning**, **functional mapping/training**, **mechanics/vascular interaction**, **exposure geometry**, and **independent prioritization/claim review**. The lead additionally researched navigation and observed anatomy updates. The independent reviewer rechecked shared evidence and challenged proposed uses; it did not supply an independent clinical validation dataset. Literature supports the stated mechanisms or experimental precedents. The proposed combinations, interfaces, curricula and acceptance experiments belong to this research plan.

### Navigation, uncertainty and observed anatomy updates — lead synthesis; independent review

- **R01 — [Geshvadi et al., “Optimizing registration uncertainty visualization to support intraoperative decision-making during brain tumor resection” (2025)][R01].** Use: Objective 10A and the display component of 9A. Access: publisher abstract and accessible manuscript excerpts. Six clinicians and three computer scientists explored visualization choices; surgeon participants preferred local probe-centered displays and explicit millimeter values. This supports usability experiments, not calibrated uncertainty, automated observation choice or improved patient outcomes.
- **R02 — [Juvekar et al., “ReMIND: The Brain Resection Multimodal Imaging Database” (2024)][R02]; [official collection](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/remind/).** Use: Objective 4A; already in the original library. Access: collection record and publication material. The 114-patient multimodal cohort includes 92 gliomas. Verify each required image pair, timing, frame and coverage. These are observations from actual operations, not outcomes for alternative simulated actions.
- **R03 — [Dorent et al., “The Brain Resection Multimodal Image Registration (ReMIND2Reg) 2025 Challenge”][R03].** Use: Objective 4A; already in Appendix A. Access: full preprint/challenge methods. Landmark-based registration accuracy, robustness and runtime provide a benchmark precedent. Check exact metric definitions and hidden-label access; the challenge design is not proof that a chosen registration method improves planning or outcomes.
- **R04 — [Rai et al., “Brainshift correction using navigated intraoperative ultrasound informs intraoperative decision-making during glioma surgery” (2025)][R04].** Use: Objective 4A. Access: full publisher text. A case-based workflow illustrates serial ultrasound updates and rigid fusion. It explicitly notes limitations with nonlinear deformation. Use it to motivate a workflow and failure scenario, not to supply a population efficacy estimate or validate general brain-shift correction.

### Functional mapping, connectivity and coaching — functional/training subagent; independent review

- **R05 — [Giglio et al., “Artificial Intelligence–Augmented Human Instruction and Surgical Simulation Performance: A Randomized Clinical Trial” (2025)][R05].** Use: Objective 6A; already in Appendix C. Access: full text. The trial randomized 88 medical students and analyzed 87; personalized human instruction informed by AI metrics improved simulator performance and transfer to a more complex NeuroVR task relative to AI tutoring alone. Feedback was real-time, with no post hoc feedback. It does not validate five-second replay, long-term retention, operating-room transfer or patient benefit.
- **R06 — [Fazlollahi et al., “AI in Surgical Curriculum Design and Unintended Outcomes for Technical Competencies in Simulation Training” (2023)][R06].** Use: Objective 6A. Access: full article. The secondary analysis motivates checking movement/efficiency alongside the safety metrics targeted by instruction. Individual effects are exploratory because of multiple comparisons without multiplicity adjustment. Use independent transfer measures and inspect unintended behavior; do not train and judge a coach solely by the same composite score.
- **R13 — [Sanai et al., “Functional outcome after language mapping for glioma resection” (2008)][R13].** Use: the awake-mapping extension of Objective 9A; already in Appendix C. Access: indexed abstract. The 250-patient clinical series motivates patient-specific language localization and mapping evidence. It does not define an arbitrary simulated patient's response to every task or establish that absent/negative evidence is safe.
- **R14 — [Erez et al., “Intraoperative mapping of executive function using electrocorticography for patients with low-grade gliomas” (2021)][R14].** Use: Objective 9A. Access: full publisher text. This three-patient feasibility study analyzed task-dependent ECoG signals offline during simple and switch counting. Recording activity is distinct from direct stimulation evidence and functional indispensability. It motivates task-specific observations, not a validated cognitive-deficit simulator.
- **R15 — [Maier-Hein et al., “The challenge of mapping the human connectome based on diffusion tractography” (2017)][R15].** Use: Objective 4B. Access: study findings reviewed; full methods must be rechecked for the chosen reconstruction experiment. The known-ground-truth challenge found substantial plausible false-positive bundles. Its results motivate competing anatomical hypotheses and topology controls; they are not accuracy estimates for current methods in an individual glioma patient.

### Tissue interaction, vascular consequences and fluid visibility — mechanics/vascular subagent

- **R07 — [Bollu et al., “Experimentally constrained circuit model of cortical arteriole networks for understanding flow redistribution due to occlusion and neural activation” (2017 online)][R07].** Use: Objective 7A. Access: full author-hosted paper. The rat cortical-network model combines imaging-derived topology with measured flow constraints and evaluates redistribution after occlusion. It supports reduced circuit models and tests of collateral dependence. Geometry alone does not supply human boundary pressures, collateral adequacy, tissue tolerance or patient infarction probability.
- **R08 — [Huang et al., “Model-Predictive Control of Blood Suction for Surgical Hemostasis using Differentiable Fluid Simulations” (2021)][R08].** Use: Objective 7B. Access: full paper. Differentiable fluid simulation and a silicone-cavity experiment with dyed water and a research robot support controlled suction-planning benchmarks. Clearing fluid does not by itself demonstrate source control, preserved perfusion or validity in brain tissue. Preserve these separate outcomes when adapting the method.
- **R20 — [Roca et al., “An expandable chamber for safe brain retraction: new technologies in the field of transcranial endoscopic surgery” (2023)][R20].** Use: retraction in Objective 6B. Access: publisher abstract and article metadata. The reported spatula experiments distinguish applied force from inclination-dependent local pressure. This motivates contact geometry and pressure maps; the proposed scheduling experiment and any clinical injury threshold need separate evidence.
- **R21 — [Hongo et al., “Monitoring retraction pressure on the brain. An experimental and clinical study” (1987)][R21].** Use: time history in Objective 6B. Access: indexed abstract. Dog experiments and clinical observations of time-dependent retraction pressure motivate release/reposition and relaxation measurements. Do not extract an assumed universally safe schedule or transfer old device/subject parameters into a modern patient model without checking methods and applicability.
- **R22 — [Sakatani et al., “Measurement of Local Heat Generation and Effects of Irrigation during Electrical Coagulation Using Bipolar Diathermy Forceps” (1995)][R22].** Use: thermal history in Objective 6B. Access: publisher abstract; full PDF retrieval was unsuccessful during review. Power, moisture, contact and irrigation dependence motivate stateful heating/cooling experiments. This is a parameter-research lead, not a source of clinical settings or a validated thermal-damage rule.

### Exposure and maneuverability — exposure-geometry subagent

- **R09 — [Houlihan et al., “Volume of Surgical Freedom: The Most Applicable Anatomical Measurement for Surgical Assessment and 3-Dimensional Modeling” (2021)][R09].** Use: Objective 5A. Access: primary article. Volumetric corridor assessment and instrument-length considerations motivate normalized comparisons. Anatomical/cadaveric measurements do not establish live bimanual feasibility, injury risk or neurological outcomes.
- **R10 — [Tariciotti et al., “Volume of Operative Maneuverability as a New Measurement in Neuroanatomical Research: A Methodological Quantitative Study and Translational Use in the Operating Room” (2025)][R10].** Use: Objective 5A. Access: indexed abstract. Seven approaches in four cadaveric specimens and one illustrative meningioma case support examining irregular entry/target surfaces and local maneuverability. Its standardized measurement uses a 10 mm target distance; this is a method definition, not a clinical safety margin.
- **R11 — [“Volumetric 3-Dimensional Analysis of the Supraorbital vs Pterional Approach to Paramedian Vascular Structures: Comprehensive Assessment of Surgical Maneuverability” (2022)][R11].** Use: Objective 5A. Access: indexed abstract. Ten pterional and ten supraorbital craniotomies were compared through target-specific angles and volumes. This supports measuring the actual target and corridor; it does not imply that a larger approach is always preferable or that cadaveric results predict glioma outcomes.
- **R12 — [“From planning to execution: Interactive virtual-reality assisted craniotomy planning in meningioma surgery” (2026)][R12].** Use: later exposure validation in Objective 5A. Access: abstract and accessible methods/results text. The 83-case study offers a planned-versus-performed opening comparison. Its 5.9% difference concerns an aggregate geometrical index; reported mean AP/lateral deviations were 17.5%/15.9%. Do not recast 5.9% as individual planning error. Meningioma opening agreement does not validate glioma maneuverability or outcomes.

### Planning, information acquisition and computational allocation — planning/learning subagent

- **R16 — [Silver and Veness, “Monte-Carlo Planning in Large POMDPs” (2010; POMCP)][R16].** Use: Objective 9A. Access: full paper. Particle beliefs and Monte Carlo tree search provide a concrete candidate for planning with incomplete observations. Its non-surgical benchmarks provide algorithmic precedent only; observation likelihoods, acquisition costs and operative dynamics need their own evidence and simpler baselines.
- **R17 — [Oberst and Sontag, “Counterfactual Off-Policy Evaluation with Gumbel-Max Structural Causal Models” (2019)][R17].** Use: Objective 9C. Access: full paper. The synthetic-sepsis demonstration motivates counterfactual policy debugging and explicit structural assumptions. Identical transition probabilities can coexist with different counterfactual mechanisms. Shared seeds alone do not resolve this ambiguity, and the paper does not identify counterfactual surgical outcomes.
- **R18 — [Hay et al., “Selecting Computations: Theory and Applications” (2012)][R18].** Use: Objective 9B. Access: full author-hosted paper. Metareasoning supplies a framework for choosing computations by expected decision value relative to cost. Adapting it to expensive native previews is the proposed experiment, requiring identical information, complete overhead accounting and retained acceptance checks.
- **R19 — [Everett et al., “Certifiable Robustness to Adversarial State Uncertainty in Deep Reinforcement Learning” (preprint 2020; linked version revised 2022)][R19].** Use: related method research for Objective 10A. Access: abstract. Bounded-state action selection and value bounds motivate studying decision stability. A certificate for a particular learned model and uncertainty set does not certify surgical geometry, absent anatomy or clinical safety. Obtain and inspect the full method before adopting its certification machinery.

### Independent-review conclusions to preserve

The independent reviewer prioritized local uncertainty/decision change, measured brain-shift replay and human-reviewed event replay as early demonstrations. It placed fluid/source-control and vascular topology among the higher-upside extensions, with calibrated mechanics, comprehensive awake behavior and patient-specific tract claims dependent on further evidence. Preserve its corrections throughout implementation: preference studies are not outcome trials; NeuroVR transfer is not operating-room transfer; real-time coaching evidence does not validate post hoc replay; tracking variation is not full reconstruction uncertainty; and an anatomy viewer does not establish that the planner consumed critical structures. These review priorities inform sequencing, now preceded by the real-data audit and expansion stages in section 11. Sections 1, 4, 9 and 12 govern data eligibility, recorded-experience learning and resolution of missing-evidence problems throughout.
