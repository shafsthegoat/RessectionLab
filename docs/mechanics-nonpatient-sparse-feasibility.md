# Non-patient tet10 sparse-solver preparation

This source-only preparation asks whether the pinned FEBio 4.13 repaired Apple Accelerate direct solver can solve an **idealized** tet10 finite-strain cube near the algebraic size of the rejected RESECT Case4 v4 mesh. It does not run a mesher or solver and does not read patient data. The baseline remains the existing geometric planner. The justification and the distinction between measured memory and dense-profile arithmetic are in `artifacts/mechanics-sparse-solver-feasibility-v1/RESULT.md`.

`manifests/experiments/nonpatient-tet10-sparse-feasibility-v1.json` freezes the seven-call order, 10 mm cube, structured six-tet subdivision levels 5/9/13, numerical Ogden gauge, boundary motions, fixed physical sample locations, residual and resource gates, and exact identities of the repaired runtime, solver profile and prior tet10 controls. The expected 1,331/6,859/19,683 nodes and 750/4,374/13,182 elements are **combinatorial predictions**, not generated-mesh observations. The gauge `mu=1000 Pa`, `K=9666.666… Pa` is inherited from a software fixture; it is not a brain modulus. Straight-reference tet10 and eight-point integration are required. The finer half-step call changes only the increment size, from four to eight equal steps.

The affine case prescribes a known deformation on the exterior and leaves the interior free. Its independent continuum oracle returns deformation Jacobian, Cauchy stress, first Piola stress, total energy and boundary virtual work. FEBio's existing fixture uses body-on-constraint reactions (`−Pn`); the work check uses that signed convention and moment balance uses **deformed** coordinates. This can catch wrong units, material law, boundary interpolation and solver output even when FEBio exits successfully. A homogeneous affine field alone cannot establish integration-point placement or distinguish quadrature rules that integrate constants identically; the future native runner must separately verify exact TET10G8 element and quadrature identity. The nonuniform case fixes the bottom, moves the top and leaves side faces traction-free. Displacement at seven fixed interior points and signed integrated top/bottom reactions are compared across all three meshes; the finest call is repeated at half the load-step size. Absolute allowances and a decreasing-difference ratio are declared before execution. A failed threshold remains a failure of this v1; no post-result tolerance or mesh change is allowed.

`scripts/mechanics_nonpatient_sparse_feasibility.py` checks declaration drift and computes only the analytic homogeneous oracle and convergence metrics on **supplied fixture arrays**. Passing its unit tests does not prove the native implementation or a solve. Before execution, a separate reviewed source slice must implement and test deterministic tet10 incidence, correct FEBio edge ordering, positive reference-element Jacobians (with volume units), dimensionless deformation Jacobians above 0.2, boundary-tag checks, an exact deck writer, native output parsing, independent force/moment balance, final residual extraction, backend-selection evidence, and source/runtime/output hashes. A separate root-authored release must bind its committed source and one fresh supervised output directory. No native run, patient solve, geometry admission or clinical interpretation is authorized by this document or manifest.

If a future supervised run completes, its strongest possible conclusion is numerical feasibility for this non-patient cube under the declared budget. The Case4 surface still failed the 2 mm geometry gate and its T1 envelope has known exclusions; patient deformation, tool force, retraction/cutting and injury remain unvalidated. Solver feasibility must not be used to turn simulated interactions into observed clinical outcomes or RL reward evidence.
