# Initial 55% host floor: source and saved-evidence audit

**Current decision:** retain the committed v1 55%/normal initial admission guard. This source audit does not authorize a lower floor, a new large predecessor read, or a native solve. The direct post-model snapshot was 50%/normal mask 1 (7,297,761,280 B used swap), so v1 correctly refuses before reserving an attempt.

The 55% number entered the read-only comparison protocol as a **matched starting condition**: the earlier unhinted diagnostic started at 55%/mask 1, then dropped to 40%/mask 2 while its outer predecessor hashes were read. The fixed comparison protocol required both arms to start at or above 55% so their host states could be compared. The single hinted arm then started at 55% and all 24 saved samples stayed at 55%/mask 1 while 14 hinted opens read 2,801,621,755 B; it passed the same checksums/size/stat validation. That is favorable feasibility evidence for the per-descriptor hint at that starting state, **not** a causal proof or a measurement of behavior starting at 50%.

The old root preflight held release when post-validation availability was 44% against an unchanged **45%/normal operational native gate**; those holds wrote no release, output or native attempt. The frozen old runner itself checks storage, output, elapsed time, source/receipt/deck/runtime identity and resource caps; the host percentages were operational guards in the root protocol and new launcher, not a mechanics, numerical-convergence or clinical threshold. In the new launcher, initial 55% is an additional versioned guard inherited from the matched read-only comparison; the pre-reservation and immediately pre-supervisor 45%/normal gates remain separate. Thus the initial 55% is conservative but has no independent measured physiological or FEBio requirement.

A 50% initial floor would leave only five percentage points, about 0.8 GiB on this 16 GiB host, above the unchanged 45% native gate. The unhinted observed 15-point fall shows why simply assuming that margin is safe would be unsound; the one hinted run at 55% does not bound a future run at 50%, especially just after a model pressure stop and with substantial swap already in use. A single current 50% snapshot is also too little evidence that 55% is a persistent bottleneck rather than transient recovery.

If 55% remains persistently unavailable while mask stays normal, a separately reviewed **read-only, one-use 50%-start feasibility arm** would be the narrow measurement before proposing a versioned v2 launcher. It would use the same exact original validator, source/deck/receipt bindings, 14 expected hinted opens, hash/stat/size checks, owned-worker containment and existing read-only caps; sample the host during and after the 2.8 GB predecessor reread, retain failures, and require the unchanged 45%/normal pre-native floor. No native output or solver would be authorized in that arm. If the minimum sampled availability falls below 45%, the mask leaves normal, source/receipt identity changes, hint coverage fails, or cleanup/caps fail, keep v1 and hold. A passing single arm supports only bounded feasibility under that host state; it still would not prove a general no-cache benefit or justify changing the original numerical/physical acceptance gates.
