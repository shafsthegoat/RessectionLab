# TRAIN-only actor architecture diagnosis

This is a read-only source and saved-output analysis. It adds no policy forward, native transition, checkpoint load, training update, SELECT/MEASUREMENT read, or new experiment. The audited 40 TRAIN states and the original failed pilot remain the evidence; the proposed full-teacher 32-update fit is a separate, prospective optimization diagnostic.

## What the actor can observe

The input includes eight public spatial values (six base images, the one-voxel public goal, and committed probe contact), each accompanied by coverage and availability. The movement score receives a 2×2×2 pooled convolutional context, normalized entry/tip/axis and tool dimensions, a stop/aspirate/probe one-hot, and sampled features and in-bounds flags along the candidate entry-to-tip ray. The goal can therefore affect both global and path-local features. Action/tool IDs are provenance, not neural features. There is no explicit goal-to-tip distance, goal-to-ray distance, or two-step reachable-probe scalar. Two 3³ convolutions provide local spatial mixing; coarse pooling can discard within-bin detail. These observations identify an inductive-bias question, not a proof that goal-relative reasoning is impossible.

The STOP score uses **only the pooled volume, procedure state, and spacing**. It does not receive movement geometry, modes, ray features, legal-action mask, or the candidate summaries supplied to the critic. The actor computes a movement score for the STOP row but discards it. In source terms, for fixed public volume/state/spacing and fixed weights, changing only the legal candidate inventory leaves the raw STOP logit unchanged, while movement logits may change. Final STOP choice may still change because it competes against those movement logits. This source-level counterfactual is not asserted to be a physically reachable pair of simulator states: real inventory changes can also change the public volume or procedure state.

## What the audited TRAIN states show

The 40 distinct teacher states contain 16 root aspirations, 8 root STOPs, and 16 second-step probes. On the 24 roots, legal aspiration counts range 1–12; probe is unavailable. On each of the 16 second-step states, at least one aspiration remains legal (1–11 legal aspirations) alongside 1–3 legal probes. Thus the probe ranking is not a single-mode inventory artifact. IL ranks the exact teacher probe first among movements in 16/16 second-step states, but the exact teacher aspiration first among movements in only 6/16 aspiration roots. STOP still exceeds the best movement by 0.071–0.103 on probe states and 0.256–0.287 on aspiration roots. The greedy IL action is STOP on all 40 states; RL has mean STOP probability 0.9999908. The initial model never greedily STOPs. Fixed-state IL cross-entropy improves 1.861780→1.822646, with movement-state CE effectively unchanged (2.053795→2.054069) and STOP-state CE improving 1.093719→0.896954.

These rows support a narrow diagnosis: there is usable movement ordering signal, especially for probe versus concurrently legal aspiration, but the separate STOP score dominates the final IL comparison. Probe labels occur only after aspiration and the mode one-hot plus procedure state can distinguish that stage, so 16/16 probe ranks do not demonstrate goal-specific geometric reasoning. STOP is 8/40 unique teacher labels and 32/128 original IL draws, so simple STOP-majority labeling is not a sufficient explanation. The source and saved rows cannot isolate underfitting, score calibration, path representation, or label/mode shortcuts as the cause. RL's near-certain STOP also has a sparse-positive exploration history and should not be assigned the IL cause by analogy.

## Smallest next interpretation gate

Keep the already specified full-40-teacher, 32-update run unchanged: original architecture, loss, masks, seed, optimizer and data, with no STOP penalty or new feature. Its fixed TRAIN readout should report STOP-minus-best-movement margins, exact movement ranks, root aspiration choices and eight genuine STOP decisions, not cross-entropy alone. If STOP dominance remains despite improved movement ranks, an inventory-aware STOP comparison becomes a concrete **new-architecture** contingency: e.g. condition STOP on a masked legal-movement summary or score STOP and movements with one calibrated action head. That would require a new architecture/checkpoint/experiment identity and fresh controls. It is not a patch to the approved fit, and no benefit is claimed in advance. Explicit goal-relative candidate distances are another possible representation change, but the present evidence does not prefer them over the smaller score-comparison mechanism.
