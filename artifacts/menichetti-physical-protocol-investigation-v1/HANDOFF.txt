Menichetti measured-validation bottleneck — public-source follow-up

Decision: the acquired release still cannot support an unqualified held-out
constitutive force-relaxation validation. The missing inputs are experimental
chronology and measurement semantics, not another numerical solver. No sealed
DEV/TEST response, MAT numeric body, fitted parameter table, or patient data was
opened; no fit or solver was run. Existing split roles remain unchanged.

What the primary sources establish

The creator release remains version 1. A fresh metadata-only file listing contains
exactly F_t_curves.mat and the fitted-statistics PDF; the MAT publisher checksum
still matches the sealed intake. Columns are force trials in micronewtons at
10 kHz, nested under brain number and region. No processing code or motion/timing
sidecar was located in the bounded search. The statistics PDF was not downloaded.
https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/7sjcn229cw/1
https://data.mendeley.com/public-api/datasets/7sjcn229cw/files?folder_id=root&version=1

The official author manuscript, methods pp.3–4, reports a 125 micrometre radius
sphere, 57.7 micrometre stage indentation at 1.65 mm/s, then a one-second hold.
Contact was established manually using microscopy and force change. The stage
used closed-loop strain-gauge positioning; its quoted specifications are not a
trial-synchronized indentation record. Ten cadaveric brains supplied regional
specimens, usually tested at five distinct sites without preconditioning. Its
supplementary-material declaration points to the same Mendeley release.
https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/9c55d8a5-7dbd-40a4-ba17-7014df1a93e0
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijengsci.2020.103355

New structural corroboration: all five exceptional column counts in the existing
structure-only inventory match manuscript section 2.3: Brain1 CB=6, CC=4,
SFCx=2; Brain2 SFCx=4; Brain3 TCx=4. All other matrices have five columns.
This strengthens the interpretation of Brain1…Brain10 as the paper's brain
numbers, with sites nested in regional specimens. It does not independently
certify donor identity or non-overlap with other releases. Keep the frozen
brain-level grouping; do not split regions or columns across roles. The separate
structure-paper-crosscheck.json records this response-free comparison.

The cited 2017 apparatus paper documents manufacturer calibration and unique
sensor gains, but its murine protocol includes an extra 10 micrometre deceleration
segment that was discarded, plus a contact-force rejection rule. These details
must NOT be transplanted into the 2020 human release. In particular, “same
apparatus” does not authenticate identical cropping or preload conventions.
https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.170894

Exact fields still missing from the inspected sources

1. Per-trial sample chronology, keyed to release checksum/brain/region/column:
   acquisition zero, contact, ramp start/end, hold start/end, and whether records
   were cropped, padded, resampled or filtered. The 10,350 rows are compatible
   with about 350 ramp samples plus 10,000 hold samples; that arithmetic is an
   inference, not an authenticated boundary. Do not choose a boundary by looking
   at protected response peaks or by minimizing model error.
2. Actual indentation versus time, or a creator-certified reconstruction with
   synchronization and error bounds: stage motion, initial contact/preload,
   probe/support compliance and hold stability. The paper supplies a reported
   nominal protocol, not these per-trial records. Controller resolution alone
   does not bound dynamic tissue indentation error.
3. Stored force semantics: compressive polarity, tare/baseline operation and
   interval, residual preload, gain/calibration and preprocessing. Mixed signs
   in the already published TRAIN validity counts do not settle these fields.
4. For an absolute force prediction, the local specimen geometry/support and
   justified contact/boundary approximation. The paper's idealized FE cylinder
   is a modeling choice, not a measured per-site specimen geometry. Brain-number
   grouping is reasonably corroborated above; cross-release donor equivalence
   remains unknown, so companion human datasets cannot be treated as independent.

Smallest defensible route

Obtain a creator-authenticated protocol/export manifest supplying items 1–3,
bound to this exact release (plus a justified geometry/boundary assumption for
absolute contact force). No outreach was performed or authorized here. Then use
the existing FEBio implementation with the authenticated ramp/hold input and
the existing brain-level split: estimate material parameters from TRAIN only,
freeze preprocessing, model, metric and acceptance limits before protected
evaluation, and compare predicted force during the certified hold on held-out
brains. Treat sites/regions within a brain as dependent when aggregating error.
Report uncertainty from the experimental input and model geometry explicitly.

Until that manifest exists, a predeclared sampled-force prediction benchmark is
possible in principle, but must be called a time-series prediction task. Neither
a guessed 350/10,000 split nor a response-normalized fit establishes a measured
constant-indentation relaxation test. No such benchmark or protected read is
authorized by this handoff.
